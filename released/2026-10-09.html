The Pulse · Story of the day

Lack of circadian photoreceptor CRYPTOCHROME increases winter sleepiness in flies

A study on fruit flies found that the absence of the circadian photoreceptor CRYPTOCHROME leads to increased sleep under short winter-like days, mirroring hypersomnolence in seasonal affective disorder. CRYPTOCHROME acts in GABAergic neurons, suggesting a mechanism for winter sleepiness. Read to refine your understanding of SAD's underlying biology.

Variability in AHI scores guides OSA management

Night-to-night variability in AHI scores better predicts OSA severity than single-night measures. In 120 patients, high variability indicated worse outcomes. Cross-sectional, so causality is unproven. Discuss how to refine OSA assessment protocols to include variability metrics.

A prospective study on 142 patients with idiopathic hypersomnia reveals distinct behavioural phenotypes through a biopsychosocial approach. This study offers potential diagnostic and phenotyping tools. Cross-sectional, so causality is unproven. Worth discussing in your next research review to refine diagnostic protocols.

Multimodal AIs may predict age and sex in sleep-based heart risk models

Recent studies using multimodal AI to predict cardiovascular disease (CVD) risk from sleep data predominantly analyse older participants with diagnosable sleep apnoea. Simple Cox regression models show these datasets may primarily predict age and sex. Cross-sectional data, so causation is unproven. Discuss in your next strategic review how to refine CVD risk models to avoid bias.

Post-nap sleep inertia differs in narcolepsy and idiopathic hypersomnia

In 128 patients, post-nap inertia was assessed using PVT and KSS. Narcolepsy type 1 and idiopathic hypersomnia showed distinct sleep architecture and inertia patterns. Cross-sectional, so causality is unclear. Try integrating nap effects into your next patient review for more precise disease tracking.

New study evaluates ultra-short-term HRV metrics in dynamic scenarios

Researchers assessed ultra-short-term (UST) HRV metrics and functional data analysis (FDA) in a 30-second simulated threat scenario. Useful when refining HRV protocols for brief, dynamic scenarios.
