OLED lighting before bed impaired next-day task performance and melatonin rise

In a randomised controlled trial, participants exposed to OLED lighting before bed showed reduced task performance and delayed melatonin secretion the following day. Cross-sectional data; useful for researchers when refining light's impact on circadian rhythms and next-day cognitive function.

Tissue corticosteroid rhythms disrupted most during sleep in primary aldosteronism

In a proof-of-concept study, tissue corticosteroid dynamics were continuously monitored in 24 primary aldosteronism patients over 24 hours using ambulatory microdialysis. Dysregulation was most pronounced during sleep, potentially impacting diagnostic and treatment strategies. Trust flag: proof-of-concept, small sample size; useful when refining diagnostic pathways for primary aldosteronism.

Extended sleep shifts physical activity timing in short-sleeping adolescents

A crossover trial of 24 short-sleeping adolescents found 1.5 hours of extra nightly sleep moved peak physical activity later by an average of 45 minutes. Randomised, so causality is strong. Worth discussing at your next adolescent sleep management review.

Later lights-off delays deep sleep and raises sleep-onset latency

In a clinical population, a greater mismatch between sleep timing and lights-off time reduced slow-wave sleep and increased time to fall asleep. Cross-sectional data; useful when refining risk stratification for sleep disorders. Discuss at your next sleep protocol review.

Structural modelling reveals key sleep determinants in preschoolers

Using structural equation modelling in 142 preschool-aged children, daytime napping and nighttime sleep duration are linked to parental sleep habits and screen time. Cross-sectional data; useful for refining sleep interventions in early childhood. Add coupling between sleep habits and screen time to your next early-childhood sleep study.

Subjective sleep quality linked to sleep continuity, not severity

A study involving 142 participants found that subjective sleep quality is more closely tied to sleep continuity than respiratory severity or sleep stage composition. Cross-sectional data. Useful when refining sleep quality assessments in clinical practice.

Pulmonary hypertension found in 10% of children with Prader-Willi Syndrome

In a cohort of 200 children with Prader-Willi Syndrome, 10% were diagnosed with pulmonary hypertension, a significant comorbidity. Cross-sectional study; useful when refining screening and management protocols for these children.

Exercise supports memory resilience. These early signals highlight how moderate exercise can bolster memory against sleep loss, suggesting a promising avenue for clinicians to explore, but more research is needed.

Moderate exercise protects memory from sleep loss via deep sleep

In 88 young adults, a single session of moderate exercise before sleep protected long-term memory from deficits caused by sleep restriction. Protection was mediated by increased deep-sleep activity. Cross-sectional study; useful for clinicians when advising on exercise for sleep-restricted patients.
