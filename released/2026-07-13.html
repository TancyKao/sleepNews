The Pulse · Story of the day

Mass spectrometry outperforms radioimmunoassay in narcolepsy hypocretin measurement

A new study shows mass spectrometry measures hypocretin levels more accurately than radioimmunoassay. In a cohort of 142 patients, the method detected lower hypocretin in over 90% with narcolepsy type 1. This shift improves diagnosis and treatment protocols for narcolepsy.

CBT-I cuts depressive symptoms long-term in insomnia patients

A meta-analysis of 14 randomised controlled trials shows CBT-I reduces depressive symptoms by an average of 0.5 standard deviations at six months post-treatment. Systematic review; useful for adjusting clinical guidelines on treating depression in insomnia.

New review highlights effective treatments for insomnia in menopausal women

A systematic review and meta-analysis of pharmacological and non-pharmacological interventions for chronic insomnia in perimenopausal and postmenopausal women finds CBT-I most effective, with low-dose antidepressants also beneficial. Useful when revising treatment protocols for this patient group.

Anthropometric indices show high sensitivity in diagnosing moderate-to-severe OSA by sex

A study using the ESADA cohort found that anthropometric measures had high diagnostic accuracy for moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnoea, stratified by sex. Cross-sectional data; useful when refining screening protocols for early identification of at-risk patients.

Digital CBT-I's acceptability can't be gauged solely by dropout rates

A letter to Sleep Medicine Reviews highlights that high attrition in digital CBT-I trials doesn’t necessarily reflect treatment acceptability. Clinicians and researchers should consider additional factors when assessing efficacy. Useful for refining inclusion criteria or measuring patient engagement beyond mere completion rates.

A qualitative study examines positional obstructive sleep apnoea (OSA) treatments in children, providing insights for clinical practice and research. No actionable methods yet; useful when revising treatment protocols for paediatric patients with positional OSA.

Body composition analysis links metabolic risk in insomnia and sleep apnea

A large study using bioelectrical impedance analysis found that patients with comorbid insomnia and sleep apnoea have higher body fat percentages, indicating increased metabolic vulnerability. Useful when refining diagnostic protocols for these conditions.

Cerebral iron buildup linked to cognitive decline in severe OSA

In a QSM study of 139 OSA patients, cerebral iron accumulation correlated with cognitive dysfunction, more pronounced in those with severe apnea (AHI > 30 events/hour). Cross-sectional data; useful when refining diagnostic protocols for cognitive impairment in OSA.
