A review and a study show mindfulness mitigates stress impact on sleep. Objective short sleep ties to higher mortality in insomnia patients

In a study of 767 adults with insomnia, those with objective short sleep duration (less than six hours) had increased mortality over eight years. Self-reported short sleep showed no such link. Cross-sectional data; useful when refining diagnostic protocols and risk stratification for sleep-related mortality.

Social jetlag linked to worse mental health and work performance in Japanese survey

A nationwide Japanese study found that social jetlag correlates with poorer mental health, increased somatic symptoms, and reduced work functioning. Cross-sectional data; useful for clinicians revising sleep disorder protocols.

First-night CPAP does not worsen sleep quality in OSA patients

A study in 142 adults with obstructive sleep apnoea found that initial positive airway pressure (CPAP) therapy did not impair sleep quality. Cross-sectional data, so causality is unproven. Useful for clinicians when initiating CPAP treatment and addressing patient concerns about initial discomfort.

Automated AOP measurement matches Doppler ultrasound across positions

A study of 46 adults found that automated arterial occlusion pressure (AOP) measurements correlated strongly with pulsed-wave Doppler ultrasound in supine, seated, and standing positions. Validity assessed via ICC and Bland–Altman analysis. Cross-sectional; useful for refining individualized blood flow restriction protocols.

Glasgow Sleep Effort Scale shows high reliability in insomnia trials

A meta-analysis of 14 studies finds the Glasgow Sleep Effort Scale has a Cronbach's alpha of over 0.8, indicating strong internal consistency for assessing insomnia efforts. Useful when selecting validated tools for clinical protocols.

Brain's navigation circuit adapts theta sweeps to moment-to-moment demands

In rats, parasubicular direction signals coordinate left-right alternating theta-paced sweeps in grid and place cells. This promotes uniform spatial coverage but whether these sweeps can be flexibly tuned was unknown. Early signal that adaptive modulation supports efficient navigation; cross-sectional data. Worth watching if this extends to other species or informs neural prosthetics.

Gene knockdown in rat pineal gland reveals regulator of melatonin synthesis

Knocking down the Cone-Rod Homeodomain transcription factor reduced melatonin levels in mature rat pineal glands. Basic research; no immediate clinical impact yet. Useful when exploring new targets for circadian rhythm disorders.

Non-invasive EIT method estimates alveolar collapse pressure in ARDS model

A proof-of-concept study in pigs with induced acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) suggests that electrical impedance tomography (EIT) can non-invasively estimate the transpulmonary pressure at which alveoli collapse. The TPP was calculated from regional pressure-flow curves during a descending ramp test. Useful when refining ventilation protocols for ARDS, but more clinical validation is needed.

PPG signals predict diabetes status using novel MFC coefficients

A study classifies HbA1c levels in 180 subjects using Mel-frequency cepstral coefficients from finger photoplethysmographic signals. The method distinguishes between normal, prediabetic, and diabetic statuses based on PPG data. Cross-sectional. Useful when exploring non-invasive diagnostic tools for diabetes screening.
